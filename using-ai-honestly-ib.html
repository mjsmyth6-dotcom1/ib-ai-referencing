Using AI honestly in your IB work

How the IB wants you to acknowledge and reference AI in your Extended Essay, IAs and TOK. You can work through this with your class or on your own.

Under the IB's rules, any AI-generated text, image or graph in your work counts as someone else's work, whether you've copied it or paraphrased it. You're allowed to use AI, but you need to be clear about where and how you've used it, and that includes AI you only used as a tool along the way.

For teachers · about 3 minutes

Ask the class what “even only in part” means for them. You could also ask whether using AI to check grammar counts as “work produced in part” by AI. The Economics IA example in the scenario sort covers this.

What the IB says

“The IB does not regard any work produced, even only in part, by such tools to be their own.”

IB Academic integrity policy · Appendix 6 · p. 54

01 · What the IB has said

What the IB has said about AI

Each of these comes from the IB's own documents. Open “Source” to see the exact wording.

AI isn't banned

The IB isn't banning AI. It expects you to use it ethically and be open about it.

Source

“The IB will not ban the use of AI software.” Academic integrity policy, Appendix 6.2, p. 53.

Crediting AI

If AI-generated text, images or graphs end up in your work, you need to credit the tool in your writing and in your bibliography.

Source

“The software must be credited in the body of the text and appropriately referenced in the bibliography.” Appendix 6, p. 54.

Prompt and date

When you cite AI in your writing, use quotation marks and include the prompt you gave it and the date of the response.

Source

“The in-text citation should contain quotation marks using the referencing style already in use by the school and the citation should also contain the prompt given to the AI tool and the date the AI generated the text.” Appendix 6, p. 55.

How you used it

If you used AI as a tool, for example to brainstorm or plan, you still need to acknowledge it. Give enough detail, including your prompts, for a reader to understand how you used it and what it added to your work.

Source

“This includes use of any artificial intelligence (AI) tools, whether used as an assistive tool or as a source of material or information used in the work. The acknowledgement should include enough detail (including the prompt/s used) to enable a reader to understand how the tool has been used and what it has contributed to the work overall.” Effective citing and referencing (IB, 2025), p. 3. The IB's scenarios document (2024) adds that “a short statement after the end of the work describing how AI was used in its creation is sufficient.”

Checking what AI tells you

AI can get things wrong or make things up. Check its information against other sources, and cite the source you used to check it.

Source

“It is important to check and validate information generated by AI, perhaps by corroborating it with information gathered from other sources.” Extended essay guide (2025), p. 94. Effective citing and referencing (2025), p. 8, says the use of the AI tool may be mentioned as part of the methodology, “and the source used to verify its output should be cited and referenced.”

Being open about it

Hiding your AI use isn't acceptable, even if what you used it for was fine. You need to tell the teacher who authenticates your work.

Source

Evaluating 13 scenarios, scenario 12. Students must disclose AI use to the teacher authenticating the work, even when no referencing is required.

For teachers · about 4 minutes

Students are most likely to miss the point about how they used AI. Brainstorming, asking for a structure or getting a concept explained still needs to be acknowledged, even if no AI words end up in the final work. A good question to ask is who they should go to if they're not sure whether to cite something. The policy says students should seek guidance from their teachers when doubts arise about referencing (Academic integrity policy, p. 46).

05 · When AI helped along the way

Acknowledging how you used AI

If you used AI as a tool, the IB still wants you to acknowledge it, with enough detail (including your prompts) for a reader to understand how you used it and what it added. The IB's scenarios document says a short statement after the end of the work is enough, and the Effective citing and referencing guide says the methodology section of a research essay may also suit. For teachers · about 4 minutes

Have students write a statement for a recent piece of work (even if they didn't use AI), then swap with a partner and check it covers the tool, the date, the prompts, what it was used for and what they did with it.

The IB doesn't give one place for it. The scenarios document suggests after the end of the work, and Effective citing and referencing suggests the methodology section of a research essay. The same guide says full AI conversations shouldn't normally go in an appendix unless students are told to include them. A school-wide format would help keep things consistent.

06 · Practice

What's wrong with this citation?

This student did cite AI in their History IA, but there are a few problems. See how many you can find before you check the answers.

History IA · draft 2
Paragraph 3

The Treaty of Versailles left Germany economically humiliated and politically unstable, which created the conditions for extremism to grow (ChatGPT).

Bibliography

ChatGPT. chatgpt.com.

  1. No quotation marks. If these are the AI's words, the IB says the citation should use quotation marks.
  2. No prompt. The IB says the citation should include the prompt.
  3. No date the response was generated.
  4. Incomplete reference. No company, year, version or web address, and it doesn't follow a recognised style.
  5. Whose thinking is it? This sentence carries the argument of the paragraph, and the IB doesn't regard any work produced by AI, even in part, as the student's own. A teacher would want to talk to this student about where the analysis came from.
  6. Did they check it? AI can get things wrong. If the student checked this against a history source, that source should be cited and referenced too (Effective citing and referencing, p. 8).

Following the IB's APA 7 examples, the corrected sentence would read “When prompted with ‘Why did the Treaty of Versailles contribute to the rise of extremism in Germany?’ on 5 May 2026, ChatGPT responded that the treaty left Germany ‘economically humiliated and politically unstable’ (OpenAI, 2026).” The reference list entry would be OpenAI. (2026). ChatGPT (May 5 version) [Artificial intelligence system]. https://chatgpt.com

07 · EE, IAs and TOK

What to keep in mind for each assessment

  • Using AI to write your essay and presenting it as your own is dishonest. Material from AI can be one of your resources, as long as you acknowledge and cite it (EE guide, p. 94).
  • Check anything AI tells you against other sources. Because AI can “hallucinate”, the guide suggests using ordinary search engines for the investigative stage of your research (EE guide, pp. 94-95).
  • Don't get AI to write your research question. Talk to your supervisor first. Once you've agreed a subject and topic, you can ask AI for example questions and then work on them with your supervisor (scenario 4).
  • Your 500-word reflective statement on the RPF, and what you say in your three reflection sessions, need to be your own. The IB treats an AI-written reflection as fabrication (scenario 11).
  • Your supervisor can read and comment on one draft only, and you need to do your own proofreading (EE guide, pp. 94 and 105).
  • Citations, references and your bibliography don't count towards the 4,000 words, but quotations do (EE guide, pp. 87-88). References for online sources must include the date you accessed them (p. 92).
  • Record source details in your RRS as you go. For AI, that means the tool, your prompt and the date (EE guide, p. 95).
  • Your supervisor confirms your essay is your own, partly through the viva voce at the end, so be ready to talk through any part of it (EE guide, p. 105, and Appendix 6, p. 54).
For teachers

These points are checked against the Extended essay guide (first assessment 2027). For more on AI, the guide refers students to Appendix 6 of the Academic integrity policy and an IB infographic, “The IB and artificial intelligence tools”. The guide doesn't say where a statement of AI use should go in the essay or whether it counts towards the word count, so check with your EE coordinator.

  • Credit any AI-generated text, images or graphs in your work (Appendix 6, p. 54).
  • In language acquisition courses such as Language B you can't use tools that improve your sentences. The IB also says you can't write in one language and translate it for submission (Appendix 6, p. 55, and scenario 8).
  • Spell checkers and bilingual dictionaries are fine in other subjects.
  • If you use a translation tool for any part of your work, mention it in your text. It doesn't usually need a reference (Effective citing and referencing, p. 8).
  • If your IA has a methodology section, that can be a good place to mention AI you used as a tool (Effective citing and referencing, p. 8).
  • The IB's AI guidance is general, so check with your subject teacher about anything specific to your subject.
For teachers

The IB's AI guidance is general and doesn't give subject-by-subject IA rules, so check your subject guide for anything specific.

  • Your arguments, examples and analysis need to be your own, because the IB doesn't regard work produced by AI, even in part, as yours.
  • Asking AI for counter-positions is fine if you then investigate them yourself (scenario 2).
  • If AI-generated text, images or graphs appear in your essay, credit them with quotation marks, the prompt and the date, using one referencing style consistently.
  • Your commentary needs to be your own work.
  • If you're thinking about using an AI-generated image as one of your objects, check with your TOK teacher first. The IB's AI guidance doesn't say whether this is allowed, and if you do use one it needs to be credited like any other AI-generated image.
  • For anything shown visually, the IB suggests a label or caption, or a short citation with the full reference listed elsewhere (Effective citing and referencing, p. 3).
For teachers

The TOK guide wasn't one of the documents this was checked against, so please confirm anything TOK-specific there.

08 · Before you hand it in

Before you hand it in

Go through these for every piece of assessed work.

Before your teacher signs off your work, the IB expects them to have seen it develop over time and to be confident you can explain it. It also needs to be clear which ideas are yours, and the quality should fit with what they know you can do (Appendix 6, p. 54). If they aren't convinced the work is yours, it can't be submitted.

For teachers · things the IB guidance doesn't cover
  • The IB doesn't require a particular referencing style. Appendix 6 (2023) mentions the style already used by the school, and Effective citing and referencing (2025) says schools should allow students to choose their own style as long as they use it consistently. ISWA uses APA 7.
  • The IB says AI used as a tool needs acknowledging, with prompts, but doesn't give a format. A statement after the end of the work or a mention in the methodology are both suggested.
  • The scenarios document (2024) suggests giving the teacher a transcript of AI use as an annex, while Effective citing and referencing (2025) says AI conversations shouldn't normally go in appendices unless instructed. This page follows the newer guide and tells students to keep their chats in case their teacher asks.
